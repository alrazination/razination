How we design a simulation

Five steps, shown with moments from the projects above.

  1. Find the moment that matters

    Not the topic. The decision where people go wrong.

  2. Make the consequence visible

    A mechanic that shows the effect, the way the real world would.

  3. Design the mistakes

    Every wrong move plays out, and comes with a reason.

  4. Build it for where learners are

    Delivered for the platforms and devices your learners already use.

  5. Play-test and tune

    Hints, difficulty and feedback, adjusted until it feels fair.

How a project runs

You see something playable early, and we agree up front how we'll know it worked.

  1. Discovery

    We find where people actually go wrong, from your material, your experts and, where possible, the job itself.

  2. Prototype

    One key decision, fully playable, before anything is built at scale.

  3. Production

    The full build, with review rounds and checks on accessibility and your platform.

  4. Launch and review

    We support the rollout, then look at how learners actually play it.

  • Playable earlyA working prototype before full production, so decisions are made on something real.
  • Accessible by designKeyboard support, readable type, and reduced-motion settings respected.
  • Checked by your expertsSubject-matter review is built into every phase, not added at the end.
  • No personal data by defaultOur simulations collect nothing about learners unless you ask for tracking.

Common questions

What do you need from us to start?

Your existing material (slides, procedures, manuals), access to one or two subject experts and, if possible, a few people from the audience to talk to or test with.

Will it work on our learning platform?

We plan delivery around the platform your learners already use, and confirm exactly how it will run during discovery.

Can you work with an existing course?

Yes. We can add a simulation to a course you already have, or redesign a module around practice.

What does a project cost?

It depends on the scope: how many decisions, the level of visual detail, and where it needs to run. After a first call you get a written proposal with scope, timeline and price.
